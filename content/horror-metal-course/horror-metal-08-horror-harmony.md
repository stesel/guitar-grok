# Horror Metal 08 — Horror Harmony: Tension Notes

**Goal:** Expand the riff vocabulary beyond D5, F5 and G5 and learn to choose notes for tension rather than simply adding more pitches.

## Setup

- Guitar Pro 8
- Drop D
- 4/4 at 170 BPM
- Rhythm guitar, bass and drums
- Keep the groove-first workflow from Lessons 1–7

## New vocabulary

On the low D string, use these roots:

- **D — fret 0:** home
- **Eb — fret 1:** maximum friction; minor second above D
- **F — fret 3:** dark/stable; minor third above D
- **G — fret 5:** open/heavy; perfect fourth above D
- **Ab — fret 6:** unstable/ominous; tritone above D

Use them as single pedal-tone accents or as Drop-D-style power-chord shapes where appropriate. Do not turn the exercise into a scale run.

## Rhythm Lab — Keep Rhythm, Change Emotion

Take one strong one-bar rhythm from Lesson 7 and copy it four times. Keep every attack and rest identical.

Change only one important chord/root in each copy:

1. D → F → D
2. D → Eb → D
3. D → G → D
4. D → Ab → D

Listen without editing the rhythm. Describe the emotional difference between the four versions.

## Core idea — Home and tension

D is the tonal anchor. The other notes have different relationships to it.

**Eb (b2)** sits only a semitone above D. It creates immediate claustrophobic friction and works especially well when it appears briefly before returning to D.

**F (b3)** gives the darker minor character already used in previous lessons. It is tense enough to add colour but stable enough to repeat frequently.

**G (4)** creates weight and movement without sounding especially dissonant.

**Ab (b5 / tritone)** is much more unstable. Use it deliberately. One well-placed Ab can be more effective than repeating it throughout the riff.

The lesson is not “more notes = more horror.” The lesson is **choosing the right amount of tension and controlling when it resolves**.

## Exercise 1 — The Semitone Threat

Write a 2-bar phrase using only D and Eb.

Requirements:

- D remains the pedal/home note.
- Eb appears no more than twice per bar.
- At least one Eb must return directly to D.
- Preserve a recognizable rhythmic hook.

Try placing Eb immediately before a strong D attack and compare that with placing Eb directly on a strong beat.

## Exercise 2 — The Tritone

Duplicate the same rhythm and replace Eb with Ab.

Do not change the rhythm.

Compare D–Eb with D–Ab. Decide which sounds more claustrophobic and which sounds more sinister or unstable to you.

## Exercise 3 — One Horror Event

Write four bars using mostly D5, F5 and G5. You may use **one** Eb5 or Ab5 in the entire phrase.

Make that chord an event through placement, silence, accent, drum support or duration. If it sounds ordinary, move it rather than adding more dissonant notes.

## Exercise 4 — A P A P' with tension

Use the four-bar structure established in Lesson 7:

```text
Bar 1 — A
Bar 2 — P
Bar 3 — A
Bar 4 — P'
```

Let A establish the recognizable hook. Use Eb or Ab primarily in P/P' so that the tension functions as an answer or payoff instead of obscuring the hook.

For eight bars, develop it as:

```text
A  P  A  P'
A  P  A' P''
```

Make P'' the strongest harmonic payoff, but do not assume it needs the largest number of notes.

## Exercise 5 — Resolve vs Don't Resolve

Create two endings for the same 4-bar riff.

- **Ending A:** tension note resolves back to D.
- **Ending B:** phrase ends on the tension note or leaves space after it.

Loop both versions. Notice how resolution feels complete while unresolved tension can pull the listener into the next section.

## Drum interaction

Continue using drums. Do not automatically place a kick under every guitar attack.

For the main horror note, try three treatments:

1. Guitar + kick together.
2. Guitar + crash/kick together.
3. Guitar alone, followed by the drums.

The third option can make a dissonant hit feel exposed and uncomfortable.

# Homework

Create **three 8-bar riffs**.

## Riff 1 — Claustrophobic

Use D as home and **Eb (b2)** as the primary tension note.

Keep Eb scarce enough that the listener notices it.

## Riff 2 — Unstable

Use D as home and **Ab (b5/tritone)** as the primary tension note.

At least one important Ab should be followed by silence or a delayed resolution.

## Riff 3 — Horror Signature

Allowed roots: D, Eb, F, G and Ab.

Write the strongest horror-metal riff you can, but preserve the Lesson 7 principle: it still needs one rhythmic hook that can be remembered independently of the pitches.

## Rules

- Tempo: **170 BPM**
- Tuning: **Drop D**
- Allowed roots: **D, Eb, F, G, Ab**
- Palm mute allowed
- Let Ring allowed
- Accents and heavy accents allowed
- Bass should support the riff
- Drums required
- No lead melody or solo
- Avoid chromatic runs; every tension note must have a compositional purpose

## Producer Challenge

After writing all three riffs, answer:

1. Which is more disturbing in your riffs: Eb or Ab?
2. Which tension note became the strongest single event?
3. Does removing the tension notes leave a good rhythmic riff underneath?
4. Which ending works better unresolved?
5. Can you still clap the main hook without thinking about pitch?

## Riff Vault

Keep the strongest riff and one alternate ending. Label the tension strategy used (`b2`, `tritone`, or `mixed`) so these riffs can later be selected deliberately when arranging the full song.
