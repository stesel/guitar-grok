# Horror Metal 09 — Riff Variation: Evolve Without Losing Identity

**Goal:** Take one strong riff and create useful variations that still sound like the same musical idea.

## Setup

- Guitar Pro 8
- Drop D
- 4/4 at 170 BPM
- Guitar, bass and drums
- Use one of your strongest riffs from Lessons 7 or 8 as the source riff

## Core idea — Identity first

A good variation should make the listener think:

> “I know this riff — but something changed.”

Not:

> “Now there is a completely different riff.”

Before changing anything, identify the source riff's **DNA**:

1. Main rhythmic hook
2. Most important accent
3. Main tension note/chord
4. Characteristic rest or gap
5. Phrase ending

Keep at least **two or three** of those elements intact in every variation.

## Rhythm Lab — One Change Only

Take one bar from your source riff and create four copies.

- Copy A: original
- Copy B: move one attack by one sixteenth note
- Copy C: remove one attack
- Copy D: move one accent or heavy accent

Do not change pitch yet.

Play all four in sequence. The goal is to hear how little material is required to create freshness.

## Exercise 1 — Density Variation

Create two versions of the same 4-bar phrase.

### Version A — Tight

Keep more palm-muted attacks and fewer long notes.

### Version B — Open

Remove approximately 20–30% of the attacks and let one or two important chords ring longer.

Keep the harmonic movement the same.

Use this test:

> Can I clap both versions and still recognize the same hook?

If not, you changed too much.

## Exercise 2 — Harmonic Variation

Keep the rhythm almost identical, but change **one tension event**.

Examples:

- F5 becomes Eb5
- F5 becomes Ab5
- A tension note resolves to D in one version but remains unresolved in another

Do not rewrite the entire chord sequence.

The purpose is to change emotional colour while preserving rhythmic identity.

## Exercise 3 — Drum-Led Variation

Keep the guitar unchanged and rewrite the drum interaction.

Create three versions:

1. **Lock:** kick follows the important guitar attacks closely.
2. **Space:** kick supports only the strongest attacks.
3. **Answer:** some kick hits happen between guitar attacks.

Listen to how the same guitar riff can feel more mechanical, heavier, or more mobile depending on the drums.

## Exercise 4 — A / A' Verse Pair

Create two related 8-bar sections:

```text
Verse A   — original riff
Verse A'  — developed version
```

A useful structure is:

```text
Verse A:
A  P  A  P'
A  P  A' P''

Verse A':
A  P  A' P'
A' P  A'' P'''
```

Do not interpret the symbols as instructions to invent new riffs. `A'` and `A''` are still descendants of A.

For Verse A', change no more than **three major elements** across the full eight bars.

Possible changes:

- One rhythm alteration
- One different tension chord
- One new ending
- Different palm-mute/open-note contrast
- Different drum support

## Exercise 5 — Vocal Space

Imagine a vocalist must sing over the riff.

Make a second version where you remove or simplify attacks during one part of each bar.

For example, preserve the hook at the beginning of the bar but leave more room in the second half.

Compare:

- Guitar-focused version
- Vocal-space version

A strong verse riff should not require the guitar to fill every available subdivision.

## Exercise 6 — The Recognition Test

Export the original and variation as audio.

Listen without looking at Guitar Pro.

Ask:

1. Can I identify which riff the variation came from?
2. Which part tells me they are related?
3. Is the variation more interesting, or merely busier?
4. Did I accidentally weaken the hook?

If the hook becomes harder to remember, undo some changes.

# Homework

Choose **one strong 8-bar riff** from Lesson 7 or 8 and create **three 8-bar variations**.

## Variation 1 — Verse Version

Make it suitable for repetition under vocals.

Requirements:

- Preserve the main rhythmic hook
- Reduce unnecessary density
- Leave intentional space
- Drums support rather than duplicate every guitar attack

## Variation 2 — Escalation Version

Make the same riff feel more intense.

You may use:

- Slightly higher rhythmic density
- Stronger accents
- More active kick pattern
- One stronger tension event
- More open power chords near the end

Do not raise intensity simply by adding notes everywhere.

## Variation 3 — Horror Version

Use the Lesson 8 vocabulary to make the source riff more disturbing.

Allowed roots:

- D
- Eb
- F
- G
- Ab

Keep the original hook recognizable.

## Rules

- Tempo: **170 BPM**
- Tuning: **Drop D**
- Guitar, bass and drums required
- Keep one source riff for all three variations
- No solos or lead melody
- Each variation must preserve at least two elements of the source riff's DNA
- Avoid changing rhythm, harmony, articulation and drums all at once

## Producer Challenge

After writing the three variations, answer:

1. Which two elements define the riff's identity most strongly?
2. Which variation changed the most while still sounding related?
3. Which change made the riff worse and had to be removed?
4. Which version would work best under vocals?
5. Which version would you use later in the same song to avoid copy-paste repetition?

## Riff Vault

Store the source riff and all three variations together as one riff family. Rate each version separately and label its intended use, for example:

- `verse`
- `verse-alt`
- `escalation`
- `horror`

This turns the Riff Vault from a collection of isolated ideas into reusable song-building material.
