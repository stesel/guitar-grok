# Horror Metal Chords for Arpeggios

## Introduction

Horror-metal arpeggios work especially well when the harmony combines **minor chords** with unstable notes and intervals such as:

- Minor seconds (♭2)
- Tritones
- Diminished chords
- Harmonic-minor dominant chords
- Chromatic chord movement
- Pedal tones

The goal is not to make every chord complicated. A simple minor chord can sound much darker when it is surrounded by tense harmony.

This lesson uses **D minor** as the main tonal center, which works especially well for guitar in **Drop D**.

---

# 1. Useful Horror Chords

| Chord | Notes | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dm | D – F – A | Dark, basic minor sound |
| Dm(add♭9) | D – E♭ – F – A | Sinister minor-second tension |
| Ddim | D – F – A♭ | Unstable, classic horror sound |
| Ddim7 | D – F – A♭ – B | Extreme tension |
| E♭m | E♭ – G♭ – B♭ | Chromatic darkness |
| B♭m | B♭ – D♭ – F | Heavy and tragic |
| A | A – C# – E | Harmonic-minor dominant tension |
| A7♭9 | A – C# – E – G – B♭ | Strong horror dominant before Dm |

The most important idea is **tension → resolution**.

A tense chord such as **A7♭9**, **Ddim7**, or **Dm(add♭9)** becomes much more effective when it eventually resolves to **Dm**.

---

# 2. Simple Creepy Progression

Try:

```text
Dm → E♭ → B♭m → A
```

Chord tones:

```text
Dm      D  F  A
E♭      E♭ G  B♭
B♭m     B♭ D♭ F
A       A  C# E
```

Arpeggiate each chord slowly instead of strumming it.

The movement from **Dm to E♭** introduces the ♭2 sound relative to D. This half-step movement is one of the easiest ways to create horror tension.

Example concept:

```text
D – F – A – F
E♭ – G – B♭ – G
B♭ – D♭ – F – D♭
A – C# – E – C#
```

Then resolve back to Dm.

---

# 3. Aggressive Horror Progression

Try:

```text
Dm → Ddim7 → E♭m → A7♭9 → Dm
```

Arpeggio note order:

```text
Dm
D – F – A – D – A – F

Ddim7
D – F – A♭ – B – A♭ – F

E♭m
E♭ – G♭ – B♭ – E♭ – B♭ – G♭

A7♭9
A – C# – E – G – B♭ – G – E – C#
```

The **A7♭9 → Dm** resolution is particularly useful because it comes directly from the sound world of D harmonic minor.

Use the A7♭9 as the peak of the tension and make the following Dm feel like a release.

---

# 4. Pedal-Tone Horror Arpeggios

Instead of changing every bass note with the chord, keep **D** underneath different upper structures.

Example:

```text
D – A  – D  – F
D – B♭ – D  – E♭
D – A♭ – C  – E♭
D – A  – C# – E
```

This creates a more modern metal sound because the low guitar remains anchored while the harmony above it becomes increasingly unstable.

Think of the low D as a **pedal tone**.

In Drop D, this is especially convenient because the open sixth string can keep ringing or repeat between upper chord tones.

---

# 5. Cinematic Horror Progression

Try:

```text
Dm → B♭m → Gdim → A → Dm
```

Chord tones:

```text
Dm      D  F  A
B♭m     B♭ D♭ F
Gdim    G  B♭ D♭
A       A  C# E
Dm      D  F  A
```

The **B♭m** is outside ordinary D natural minor and gives the progression a darker cinematic shift.

Then **Gdim → A → Dm** builds tension toward the resolution.

---

# 6. Strong Horror Formula

A particularly effective progression is:

```text
Dm(add♭9) → B♭m → Ddim7 → A7♭9 → Dm
```

Why it works:

1. **Dm(add♭9)** immediately introduces the E♭ against D.
2. **B♭m** creates unexpected chromatic color.
3. **Ddim7** removes tonal stability.
4. **A7♭9** creates maximum dominant tension.
5. **Dm** finally resolves the phrase.

You do not need to play every note of every chord. Selecting three or four important chord tones is often enough.

---

# 7. Arpeggio Writing Tips

## Do Not Always Play Root → 3rd → 5th

Instead of:

```text
D – F – A – D
```

try rearranging the notes:

```text
D – A – F – E♭
```

or:

```text
A – D – E♭ – F
```

Changing the order can make an ordinary chord sound much less predictable.

## Emphasize Minor Seconds

For D-based horror harmony, important half-step movements include:

```text
D ↔ E♭
C# ↔ D
A ↔ B♭
```

These intervals immediately create tension.

## Use Wide Intervals

Do not keep all chord tones adjacent.

For example:

```text
D – A – E♭ – F
```

can sound more cinematic than:

```text
D – E♭ – F – A
```

## Repeat One Note While Harmony Changes

Example:

```text
D – A – D – F
D – B♭ – D – E♭
D – A♭ – D – F
D – A – D – C#
```

The repeated D gives the listener something stable while everything around it changes.

# 8. Practice Exercise

Write an 8-bar clean or lightly distorted arpeggio section in D minor.

Use this structure:

```text
Bars 1–2: Dm or Dm(add♭9)
Bars 3–4: B♭m
Bars 5–6: Ddim7
Bar 7:    A7♭9
Bar 8:    Dm
```

Rules:

- Use 3–5 notes per chord.
- Include at least one **minor-second movement**.
- Keep a low D pedal tone somewhere in the progression.
- Do not use exactly the same arpeggio shape for every chord.
- Let at least one tense note ring against another note.
- Resolve clearly to Dm at the end.

Then repeat the same section with distortion and add palm-muted low-D hits between some of the arpeggio notes.

---

# Final Rule

For horror-metal arpeggios, think:

```text
Minor chord
+
♭2 / diminished / chromatic tension
+
repetition or pedal tone
+
strong resolution
```

A useful starting progression is:

```text
Dm(add♭9) → B♭m → Ddim7 → A7♭9 → Dm
```

Do not try to make every chord frightening by itself. Let the **movement between chords** create the horror atmosphere.
